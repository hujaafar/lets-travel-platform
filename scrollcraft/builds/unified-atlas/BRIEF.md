## Supplied direction and authored response

The user identified a real continuity problem: the orbital opening used black space, the lower itinerary used green, and administration, collection, forms and login retained paper and vermilion treatments. The response is one dark atlas across the entire application, not a new opening placed above an unchanged dashboard.

1. Vibe: expansive, precise, editorial and composed. Ink surfaces and warm ivory type make the photographs and copper controls stand out.
2. Product grammar: one persistent navigation frame, one operational heading component and one family of surfaces, borders, buttons, controls and feedback states. The Home scenes retain distinct motion devices while sharing the same surrounding palette.
3. Sequence: orbital Earth → route → destination arrival → departure desk → totals → itinerary → saved journeys → next-journey action. Working Travel plans, People, Payments, Calendar and Settings pages continue the same visual language.
4. Feeling curve: wonder at the orbital opening, anticipation during the descent, orientation at the desk, immersion in the itinerary, discovery in the gallery, then confidence in the editing tools.
5. Peak: the Earth-to-photograph arrival remains the primary immersive moment. Administrative navigation uses short entry motion and a quiet orbit illustration rather than repeating the peak.
6. Tell-someone sentence: “The travel atlas still feels like the same product when I leave the globe and start managing a journey.”
7. Assets: existing local destination photography, attributed NASA Blue Marble texture, original globe renderer, SVG geometry, TP monogram, DM Sans and Fraunces. No copied reference branding, source, audio or new generated assets are required.
8. Motion policy: always enabled, explicitly requested by the user. Old stored off choices and OS reduced-motion preference do not disable the scenes. No motion toggle remains. Native input, chapter controls, skip-to-workspace and keyboard navigation are retained.
