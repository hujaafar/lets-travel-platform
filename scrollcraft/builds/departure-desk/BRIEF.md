# The Departure Desk

Self-authored under explicit creative delegation. The user is going to sleep and asks for a much stronger design, real scroll effects, and use of the GitHub Scroll Craft skill. This revision replaces the previous direction, which the user rejected.

## Evidence and authored decisions

1. User: “MAKE DESGIN LIKE PRO NOT AI” and “REAL REAL DESIFN SCROLL EFFECTA”. Authored direction: a travel editor's working desk. Spacious but specific, photographic, typographic, tactile. Fraunces is used consistently for the journal's display voice because this surface presents itineraries as editorial travel stories; DM Sans handles operational labels and forms.
2. Sequence: departure desk → upcoming journey → workspace totals → a destination-by-destination itinerary → other saved plans → next planning action. These are authored decisions, not an interview transcript.
3. Energy: an arresting typographic opening, quieter operational totals, one sustained interactive itinerary, then a compact closing action.
4. Emotion: anticipation from the photographed next departure; orientation from real totals; understanding as scrolling moves a bookmark through actual stops; curiosity from the other saved journeys; agency through the real editor.
5. Signature: the moving itinerary bookmark. While the visitor reads each stop, a sticky photographic page updates its destination, route line, stop marker and visual crop. The stop buttons also navigate directly to that content. All detail comes from the saved travel record.
6. Range: editorial working desk. Off-white, deep charcoal, a single vermilion accent, strong asymmetric spreads, restrained borders and smaller radii. No default green metric-card grid.
7. Distinct spreads with one sticky reading sequence. No unbroken generated flight, no scroll hijacking, no video required.
8. Assets: the existing, locally stored destination photographs; local fonts; semantic SVG marks and route geometry. No supplied brand identity and no invented testimonials, revenue, coordinates or bookings.

## Grammar and structural constraints

Custom grammar: editorial working desk. A compact application rail stays available; the overview is a sequence of authored editorial spreads; operational pages retain focused forms and tables. The hero puts typography on paper and a photograph in its own framed region. The itinerary is the sole sustained sticky passage. The ending opens an actual planning form. Bans: fullscreen marketing navigation, repeated card grids, artificial empty pin spans, decorative statistics, full-frame dark overlays, and blocking access to application functions behind the scroll sequence.

This differs from pure chaptered editorial because it needs functional app navigation and a sticky reading tool; it differs from pure live surface because the user's correction explicitly asks for an expressive presentation and motion.

## Feeling curve and peak

Anticipation → orientation → understanding → curiosity → agency. Peak: “It's the workspace where a journey unfolds in front of me as I read its stops.” The itinerary spread receives the longest meaningful reading span, with each stop's activities, stay and transport present. There is no authored blank scroll.

## Layer contract

The cover photograph translates within its crop at the slowest rate. The photo frame remains in native document flow. The foreground departure ticket travels slightly faster in the opposite direction; its printed elements stay together. The circular trip seal rotates subtly. Headline and controls remain stable and readable. The image is not a fake multi-layer landscape, and no subject is duplicated or extracted.

On phones the ticket becomes a normal attached caption, the itinerary becomes a direct reading list, and all essential information stays visible. Reduced motion removes transforms, reveals and extra sticky space. Native scroll and keyboard links still navigate each stop.
